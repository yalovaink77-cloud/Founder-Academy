# Go-To-Market and Distribution Sprint

## Purpose

Create a testable path from the right customer to product discovery, understanding, trial, and first value.

## Sprint Question

> How will the right customers repeatedly discover and choose this product?

## Inputs

- Customer evidence.
- Positioning and message hierarchy.
- Pricing hypothesis.
- Beta/first-user evidence.
- Product activation path.

## Step 1 — Define the Initial Market

Specify:

- Priority customer/problem segment.
- Situation or trigger.
- Geography when relevant.
- Purchase/adoption context.
- Why this segment is reachable now.

Start narrow enough to learn.

## Step 2 — Map the Customer Journey

```text
Problem/Trigger
↓
Discovery
↓
Evaluation
↓
Trust
↓
Try/Buy
↓
First Value
↓
Return
```

For each step identify the main question or friction.

## Step 3 — Generate Channel Candidates

Consider channels according to customer behavior:

- Search.
- Communities.
- Direct outreach.
- Partnerships.
- Referrals.
- App/marketplace discovery.
- Content.
- Email/audience.
- Press/earned media.
- Influencers/creators.
- Paid acquisition later when ready.

Do not select a channel because it is fashionable.

## Step 4 — Score Channels

For each candidate estimate:

- Customer presence.
- Reachability.
- Speed of learning.
- Cost.
- Trust fit.
- Measurement ability.
- Repeatability.
- Founder effort.

Select a small number to test.

## Step 5 — Define Channel Experiments

Each experiment needs:

- Audience.
- Message/offer.
- Channel.
- Action requested.
- Measurement.
- Time/resource boundary.
- Continue/stop criterion.

Change as few major variables as practical so learning remains interpretable.

## Step 6 — Build the Conversion Path

Ensure the transition from channel to product is coherent:

- Message promise matches landing/store/product experience.
- Trust signals are present.
- Call to action is clear.
- Conversion can be measured.
- Activation can be connected back to source where practical.

## Step 7 — Run and Measure

Track appropriate signals such as:

- Qualified reach.
- Response/click/visit.
- Signup/start.
- Activation.
- First value.
- Purchase.
- Return.
- Cost or founder effort.

Do not optimize top-of-funnel volume while downstream value is broken.

## Step 8 — Founder Decision

For each channel:

- Continue.
- Iterate.
- Pause.
- Stop.

Record why.

## Required Outputs

- Initial market definition.
- Customer journey.
- Channel shortlist and scores.
- GTM hypothesis.
- Experiment cards.
- Conversion path.
- Distribution scorecard.
- Founder decisions.

## Exit Criteria

At least one acquisition path has been tested end to end, or the sprint has produced clear evidence about why current paths fail.

The founder knows what channel will be tested next and what evidence will determine whether it deserves more effort.

## AI Role

AI may research channels, analyze competitors, prepare experiment options, draft messages, organize results, compare channel performance, and identify bottlenecks.

Founder owns market priority, brand promises, budget/risk boundaries, and strategic channel commitment.
