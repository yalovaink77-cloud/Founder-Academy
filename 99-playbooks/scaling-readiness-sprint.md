# Scaling Readiness Sprint

## Purpose

Determine which parts of a working business are stable enough to scale, which constraints will break first, and where founder time, automation, delegation, or capital should be applied.

## Sprint Question

> What can grow now without destroying quality, economics, trust, or founder capacity?

## Inputs

- Product/customer evidence.
- Retention and growth evidence.
- Revenue/economic evidence.
- Current operating processes.
- Founder workload.

## Step 1 — Define What “Scale” Means

Specify the intended increase:

- Customers/users.
- Transactions.
- Geography.
- Product lines.
- Revenue.
- Team.
- Content/output.
- Infrastructure.

Scale is not a generic goal.

## Step 2 — Identify Proven Systems

Classify important processes:

### Proven and Repeatable

Works consistently enough to increase volume.

### Working but Fragile

Produces value but depends on manual intervention, one person, or unstable conditions.

### Unproven

Still requires experimentation.

Do not scale an unproven process merely by automating it.

## Step 3 — Find the Bottlenecks

Inspect:

- Founder time.
- Customer support.
- Product reliability.
- Acquisition capacity.
- Activation/retention.
- Fulfillment/operations.
- Cash flow.
- Technical infrastructure.
- Quality control.
- Trust/safety/compliance where relevant.

Estimate which constraint breaks first if volume increases.

## Step 4 — Choose the Leverage Mechanism

For each important bottleneck consider:

### Improve

Fix the process itself.

### Standardize

Create a repeatable procedure or quality standard.

### Automate

Use software/AI when the process is stable, measurable, and safe to automate.

### Delegate

Transfer repeatable work with clear outcomes and controls.

### Hire

Add durable human capability when the constraint justifies ongoing cost and responsibility.

### Partner

Use an external capability when ownership is unnecessary or inefficient.

### Invest

Allocate capital when expected return and downside are understood.

## Step 5 — Protect Quality and Guardrails

Define what must not deteriorate during scale.

Examples:

- Customer outcome.
- Retention.
- Support quality.
- Safety.
- Reliability.
- Margin.
- Brand trust.
- Founder strategic time.

Create guardrail metrics where possible.

## Step 6 — Evaluate Economics

Before material expansion, understand:

- Incremental revenue/value.
- Incremental costs.
- Cash requirements.
- Payback/return logic where meaningful.
- Downside if growth is weaker than expected.
- Reversibility.

Scale should not create hidden insolvency or uncontrolled commitments.

## Step 7 — Build the Scaling Sequence

Do not scale everything simultaneously.

Define:

1. Current constraint.
2. Intervention.
3. Expected capacity increase.
4. Verification metric.
5. Next likely constraint.

This creates a constraint-driven scaling loop.

## Step 8 — Founder Decision

Choose:

- Scale the proven system.
- Strengthen the fragile system first.
- Automate/delegate a defined process.
- Allocate capital.
- Delay scale and continue learning.

## Required Outputs

- Scale objective.
- Proven/fragile/unproven process map.
- Bottleneck map.
- Leverage choices.
- Guardrail metrics.
- Economic/risk view.
- Scaling sequence.
- Founder decision.

## Exit Criteria

The founder knows:

- What is being scaled.
- Why it is ready.
- What will break first.
- Which leverage mechanism will address it.
- What quality/economic guardrails must hold.
- What evidence will determine the next scaling decision.

## AI Role

AI may map processes, analyze bottlenecks, identify automation candidates, compare scenarios, monitor guardrails, and document systems.

Founder owns capital allocation, organizational commitments, risk acceptance, and strategic scale decisions.
