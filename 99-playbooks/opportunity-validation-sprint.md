# Opportunity Validation Sprint

## Purpose

Determine whether a problem is worth further founder investment before committing serious build, marketing, or capital resources.

## Entry

Use when:

- An idea is new.
- An existing product was built without strong market evidence.
- A major new segment or problem is being considered.

## Sprint Question

> Is there enough real evidence that this problem matters to a reachable customer?

## Inputs

- Problem hypothesis.
- Customer hypothesis.
- Existing research, if any.
- Known alternatives.

## Step 1 — Write the Assumptions

Record the important assumptions behind the opportunity:

- Who experiences the problem?
- What happens today?
- How often?
- What does it cost in time, money, risk, frustration, or missed opportunity?
- What alternatives are used?
- Why might someone change behavior?
- What evidence would prove the idea weak?

Rank assumptions by how damaging they would be if false.

## Step 2 — Gather Existing Evidence

Use credible existing sources before creating new research.

Look for:

- Observable behavior.
- Existing spending.
- Existing workarounds.
- Search or community demand.
- Competitors and substitutes.
- Complaints and unmet needs.

Separate facts from interpretations.

## Step 3 — Talk to the Market

Seek real customer evidence.

Prefer questions about actual past behavior over hypothetical future promises.

Useful areas:

- Last time the problem occurred.
- What triggered action.
- What they tried.
- What happened.
- What they spent or sacrificed.
- Why existing alternatives were sufficient or insufficient.

Do not sell the product during problem discovery.

## Step 4 — Test the Riskiest Assumption

Choose the cheapest ethical experiment that can create meaningful evidence.

Possible experiments:

- Interviews.
- Concierge/manual service.
- Prototype test.
- Landing-page interest test.
- Waitlist with meaningful commitment.
- Preorder or paid pilot where appropriate and truthful.
- Community outreach.

The experiment must test a stated hypothesis.

## Step 5 — Record Evidence

For each evidence item record:

- Source.
- Observed behavior or fact.
- Which assumption it affects.
- Strength: weak / medium / strong.
- Direction: supports / contradicts / unclear.

Do not turn one enthusiastic comment into market proof.

## Step 6 — Founder Decision

Choose one:

### Continue

Evidence justifies the next stage.

### Repeat

The problem may exist but evidence is too weak or broad.

### Change

Customer, problem, use case, or value hypothesis needs revision.

### Stop

Evidence does not justify further investment.

Stopping is a successful validation outcome when it prevents wasted work.

## Required Outputs

- Problem statement.
- Customer hypothesis.
- Critical assumptions.
- Evidence log.
- Experiment result.
- Founder decision.
- Next question.

## Exit Criteria

Do not advance until the founder can answer:

- Who appears to have the problem?
- What evidence shows it is real?
- What do they do today?
- Why might change matter?
- What is still uncertain?
- Why is the next investment justified?

## AI Role

AI may:

- Research evidence.
- Prepare interview questions.
- Organize findings.
- Challenge assumptions.
- Design experiment options.
- Summarize patterns.

Founder owns:

- Which opportunity to pursue.
- Ethical/risk boundaries.
- Continue/change/stop decision.

## Durable Record

At sprint end preserve:

```text
Hypothesis
Evidence
Experiment
Result
Decision
Next Action
```
