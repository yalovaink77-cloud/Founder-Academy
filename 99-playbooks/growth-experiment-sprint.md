# Growth Experiment Sprint

## Purpose

Identify the current growth constraint and run focused experiments that improve customer value and sustainable business outcomes.

## Sprint Question

> What is the highest-leverage constraint preventing healthy growth right now?

## Inputs

- Funnel and metric baselines.
- Retention evidence.
- Acquisition/channel evidence.
- Revenue/economic evidence.
- Current product/customer learning.

## Step 1 — Diagnose Before Ideating

Determine where growth is constrained:

- Acquisition.
- Conversion.
- Activation.
- Retention.
- Revenue.
- Referral.
- Capacity/operations.

Do not begin with random growth tactics.

## Step 2 — Protect the Foundation

Before scaling acquisition, verify that:

- Users can reach value.
- Important retention problems are understood.
- Measurement works.
- The offer is coherent.
- Economics are at least observable.

More traffic into a broken system is not healthy growth.

## Step 3 — Build the Experiment Backlog

Each candidate experiment should state:

- Problem/bottleneck.
- Hypothesis.
- Proposed change.
- Target metric.
- Expected mechanism.
- Effort/cost.
- Risk.
- Time to learn.

## Step 4 — Prioritize

Prefer experiments with:

- High expected impact.
- Strong connection to the diagnosed bottleneck.
- Reasonable effort.
- Fast learning.
- Clear measurement.
- Acceptable downside.

The prioritization method may vary; consistency matters more than a fashionable scoring formula.

## Step 5 — Run One Interpretable Test

Define:

- Baseline.
- Change.
- Audience/cohort.
- Primary metric.
- Guardrail metrics.
- Observation window.
- Stop rule.

Avoid mixing multiple unrelated changes when interpretation matters.

## Step 6 — Analyze the Result

Ask:

- Did the target metric move?
- Did guardrails worsen?
- Was the result large enough to matter?
- Was the mechanism consistent with the evidence?
- Is more data required?
- Did a different bottleneck become visible?

## Step 7 — Look for a Growth Loop

Where relevant, map whether product value itself can generate future acquisition or expansion.

Examples:

- Referral.
- User-generated content.
- Collaboration/network effects.
- Content/search accumulation.
- Marketplace liquidity.
- Reinvestment of profitable acquisition.

A loop must have a real reinforcing mechanism; a diagram alone is not a growth loop.

## Step 8 — Founder Decision

Choose:

- Adopt the change.
- Iterate.
- Revert.
- Run a stronger test.
- Address a different bottleneck.
- Pause scaling.

## Required Outputs

- Growth diagnosis.
- Experiment backlog.
- Priority decision.
- Experiment card.
- Result analysis.
- Growth-loop map when relevant.
- Founder decision.

## Exit Criteria

The sprint ends with evidence about a specific growth mechanism and a clear next decision.

Success is learning that improves sustainable growth, not merely increasing one vanity metric.

## AI Role

AI may analyze funnels/cohorts, identify bottlenecks, generate experiments, prioritize options, check measurement, and synthesize results.

Founder owns risk, resource allocation, brand constraints, and strategic growth choices.
