# Project Startup Protocol

## Purpose

Give Founder Academy one reusable way to start working on any real project without forcing every project to begin from zero or letting a technically mature product skip unresolved business evidence.

## Core Rule

> Start at the earliest important evidence gap, not automatically at the beginning and not automatically at launch.

---

## Step 1 — Identify the Project

Record only the minimum context:

- Project name.
- Product/business type.
- Current product state.
- Current business state.
- Current founder goal.

Do not copy the full project history into Founder Academy.

---

## Step 2 — Run the Lifecycle Audit

Evaluate evidence for each stage:

1. Opportunity / problem.
2. Validation.
3. Customer research.
4. Product/value strategy.
5. Positioning/pricing.
6. Beta/first users.
7. GTM/distribution.
8. Launch.
9. Analytics/revenue.
10. Growth.
11. Advertising readiness.
12. Scaling.

For each stage mark:

### PROVEN

Required evidence exists and is current enough.

### PARTIAL

Some evidence exists but an important uncertainty remains.

### UNKNOWN

No reliable evidence exists.

### NOT YET RELEVANT

The stage is intentionally premature.

Do not mark a stage PROVEN because documents exist. Evidence must support the stage exit criteria in `00-foundation/founder-capability-curriculum.md`.

---

## Step 3 — Find the Earliest Consequential Gap

Choose the earliest PARTIAL or UNKNOWN stage that could invalidate later work.

Examples:

- A finished app with no customer evidence starts at validation/customer research, not launch.
- A product with real retained users but weak acquisition may start at GTM/distribution.
- A business with reliable organic acquisition and retention but no paid measurement may start at advertising readiness.

---

## Step 4 — Define One Active Sprint

Use the matching playbook.

The active sprint should have:

- One main question.
- Required evidence.
- Required outputs.
- Founder decision.
- Exit criteria.

Do not run every Academy module simultaneously.

---

## Step 5 — Preserve Project Independence

Founder Academy provides the framework.

Project-specific facts, metrics, decisions, and sensitive information remain in the project's own source-of-truth system when one exists.

Founder Academy may record:

- Which framework was applied.
- Reusable lessons.
- General improvements to the playbook.

It should not become a duplicate database of every project.

---

## Step 6 — Complete the Sprint

Follow the Founder AI Operating Protocol:

```text
Goal
↓
Context
↓
Execution
↓
Verification
↓
Founder Decision
↓
Durable Record
↓
Next Action
```

---

## Step 7 — Update the Lifecycle Audit

After the sprint:

- Update the stage status.
- Record the evidence reference.
- Record the founder decision.
- Identify the next earliest consequential gap.

---

---

## Completion Standard

The startup protocol is complete when the founder can answer:

- Where this project really is in the business lifecycle.
- What is already proven.
- What is merely assumed.
- Which single sprint should run next.
- What evidence will allow the project to advance.

---

## Final Principle

> A completed product is not the same as a completed business stage. Founder Academy follows evidence, not code completion.
