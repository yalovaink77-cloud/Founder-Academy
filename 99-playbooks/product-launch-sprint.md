# Product Launch Sprint

## Purpose

Release a product in a controlled way that can acquire users, observe behavior, collect feedback, generate revenue when applicable, and create a measurable learning loop.

## Sprint Question

> Are we ready to put this product in front of the market and learn from what actually happens?

## Inputs

- Validated target customer/problem.
- Positioning and message hierarchy.
- Pricing hypothesis when applicable.
- Beta evidence.
- GTM/channel plan.
- Analytics capability.

## Step 1 — Define the Launch Objective

Choose the primary learning or business objective.

Examples:

- Acquire the first public users.
- Test a channel.
- Test positioning.
- Test conversion.
- Generate first revenue.
- Establish an activation baseline.

A launch cannot optimize everything at once.

## Step 2 — Define Success and Failure Signals

Specify before launch:

- Primary metric.
- Supporting metrics.
- Qualitative feedback needed.
- Operational failure signals.
- Continue/iterate/stop thresholds where useful.

Avoid judging launch success by attention alone.

## Step 3 — Run the Readiness Gate

Confirm:

### Product

- Core experience works.
- Critical blockers are resolved.
- First value is reachable.

### Trust and Safety

- Required policies/disclosures exist.
- Important claims are supportable.
- Support/contact path exists.

### Commercial

- Offer is clear.
- Pricing/payment path works when relevant.

### Distribution

- Initial audience and channels are defined.
- Launch messages/assets are ready.

### Measurement

- Acquisition/source can be observed where practical.
- Activation and conversion events are defined.
- Feedback capture is ready.

### Operations

- Founder knows how to respond to failures, questions, and feedback.

Do not launch blind simply because a date was chosen.

## Step 4 — Create the Launch Brief

Include:

- Audience.
- Core promise.
- Offer.
- Proof/trust points.
- Channels.
- Launch actions.
- Owner/responsibility.
- Metrics.
- Risks.
- Response plan.

## Step 5 — Execute

Release through the selected channels.

Keep an event log of meaningful actions so outcomes can be interpreted later.

Avoid changing many strategic variables during the observation window unless a serious problem requires intervention.

## Step 6 — Observe

Measure the path:

```text
Reach
↓
Visit / Response
↓
Start / Signup
↓
Activation / First Value
↓
Purchase when relevant
↓
Return / Retention Signal
```

Collect qualitative feedback alongside behavior.

## Step 7 — Respond

Prioritize:

1. Safety/security/critical trust problems.
2. Product blockers.
3. Conversion/activation friction.
4. Important user confusion.
5. Nice-to-have improvements.

Do not let launch-day requests automatically redefine product strategy.

## Step 8 — Post-Launch Review

Record:

- What we expected.
- What happened.
- Which channel/message worked or failed.
- Where users dropped.
- What users valued.
- Revenue evidence when relevant.
- Main surprises.
- Next hypothesis.

## Founder Decision

Choose:

- Continue current launch motion.
- Fix and relaunch an experiment.
- Change channel/message/offer.
- Return to product/positioning work.
- Pause broader acquisition.

## Required Outputs

- Launch objective.
- Readiness decision.
- Launch brief.
- Measurement baseline.
- Event/result log.
- Post-launch review.
- Founder decision.

## Exit Criteria

The launch has produced interpretable evidence and a clear next decision.

A launch is not complete merely because the product became public.

## AI Role

AI may prepare launch assets, run readiness checks, organize channel execution, monitor available data, synthesize feedback, and prepare the post-launch review.

Founder owns public promises, strategic tradeoffs, budget/risk boundaries, and consequential launch decisions.
