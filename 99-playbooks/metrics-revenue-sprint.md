# Metrics and Revenue Sprint

## Purpose

Build the minimum measurement and economic system required to understand product behavior, customer value, and business sustainability.

## Sprint Question

> What must we measure to make the next important product and business decisions with evidence?

## Inputs

- Customer journey.
- Product activation definition.
- Acquisition channels.
- Pricing/revenue model.
- Current analytics or transaction data.

## Step 1 — Define the Decision Questions

Do not begin with every metric that can be collected.

Start with decisions such as:

- Are users reaching first value?
- Do they return?
- Which acquisition sources produce valuable users?
- Where does conversion fail?
- Are customers paying?
- Is the revenue model economically sustainable?

## Step 2 — Define the Core Funnel

Adapt to the business, but normally map:

```text
Acquisition
↓
Activation
↓
Retention
↓
Revenue
↓
Referral / Expansion
```

Define what each stage means operationally.

## Step 3 — Create the Metric Dictionary

For every important metric record:

- Name.
- Business question it answers.
- Exact definition.
- Numerator/denominator when relevant.
- Time window.
- Data source.
- Known limitations.

Avoid changing definitions silently.

## Step 4 — Establish Baselines

Create initial baselines for the metrics that matter now.

Possible examples:

- Visitor-to-start conversion.
- Activation rate.
- Time to first value.
- Retention by cohort.
- Purchase conversion.
- Average revenue per customer/user when relevant.
- Refund/cancellation/churn.
- Acquisition cost when paid or measurable effort exists.

A baseline can be small and imperfect as long as its limitations are known.

## Step 5 — Build the Economic View

Depending on the business, calculate or estimate:

- Revenue.
- Gross margin/contribution margin.
- Variable costs.
- Customer acquisition cost when meaningful.
- Customer lifetime value only when sufficient retention/revenue evidence exists.
- Payback period when meaningful.
- Cash burn/runway for businesses with material operating costs.

Do not use precise-looking LTV estimates when the underlying retention data is immature.

## Step 6 — Create the Founder Scorecard

Keep a small scorecard tied to current decisions.

Each metric should have:

- Current value.
- Previous/baseline value.
- Direction.
- Interpretation.
- Decision threshold when appropriate.

The scorecard exists to guide action, not to decorate dashboards.

## Step 7 — Diagnose the Bottleneck

Ask:

- Is discovery weak?
- Is conversion weak?
- Is activation weak?
- Is retention weak?
- Is monetization weak?
- Are unit economics weak?

Work on the largest consequential constraint rather than optimizing every number.

## Step 8 — Founder Decision

Choose the next decision or experiment based on the evidence.

Examples:

- Fix activation before buying traffic.
- Improve retention before scaling acquisition.
- Test pricing.
- Change channel.
- Improve conversion.
- Reduce variable cost.
- Gather more data before making a major economic decision.

## Required Outputs

- Decision questions.
- Funnel definition.
- Metric dictionary.
- Baseline scorecard.
- Revenue/economic baseline.
- Bottleneck diagnosis.
- Founder decision.

## Exit Criteria

The founder can answer:

- How customers enter the system.
- Whether they reach value.
- Whether they return.
- Whether/how money is generated.
- What the current largest measurable constraint is.
- Which metric will determine the next decision.

## AI Role

AI may define candidate metrics, check calculation consistency, analyze cohorts/funnels, identify anomalies, summarize economics, and prepare decisions.

Founder owns which business outcomes matter, acceptable economics, and major resource decisions.
