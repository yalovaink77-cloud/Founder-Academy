# Beta and First Users Sprint

## Purpose

Put the product in front of relevant real users, verify that they can reach value, and create the first evidence loop before broad launch or paid scale.

## Sprint Question

> Can real users discover, start, understand, and receive the intended value from this product?

## Inputs

- Target customer.
- Value hypothesis.
- Critical user journey.
- Current product/prototype.
- Success criteria.

## Step 1 — Define the Beta Learning Goals

Choose the smallest set of questions that matter now.

Examples:

- Can users start without founder explanation?
- Can they reach first value?
- Where do they get confused?
- What creates trust or distrust?
- What makes them return?
- What blocks willingness to pay?

Beta is for learning, not vanity growth.

## Step 2 — Recruit Relevant Users

Prefer people who match the problem behavior and target context.

Possible sources:

- Existing network.
- Relevant communities.
- Professional groups.
- Customer-research participants who opted in.
- Direct outreach.
- Waitlist.
- Partners.

Do not optimize for large numbers before learning quality.

## Step 3 — Observe the Critical Journey

Capture:

- How they arrived.
- What they expected.
- Where they hesitated.
- Where they failed.
- Whether they reached first value.
- What happened afterward.

When possible, behavior should accompany stated feedback.

## Step 4 — Collect Structured Feedback

Ask about:

- Most useful outcome.
- Confusing or difficult moments.
- Missing trust signals.
- Existing alternative.
- What would make them return.
- What would make them stop using it.

Separate product problems from requests for extra features.

## Step 5 — Prioritize Issues

Classify findings:

### Blocking

Prevents access, trust, safety, activation, or core value.

### Important

Meaningfully reduces adoption or repeated value.

### Later

Useful but not necessary for the current hypothesis.

Fix blockers before adding optional scope.

## Step 6 — Build the First-User Acquisition Record

For every recruited user, record the acquisition source.

Track:

- Source/channel.
- Outreach/message.
- Response.
- Start/signup.
- Activation/first value.
- Return/retention signal.
- Qualitative learning.

This begins distribution learning before paid advertising.

## Step 7 — Founder Decision

Choose:

- Continue beta.
- Fix blockers and retest.
- Change onboarding/value path.
- Change target segment.
- Prepare controlled launch.
- Return to an earlier stage.

## Required Outputs

- Beta objective.
- User cohort.
- Critical-journey observations.
- Feedback log.
- Prioritized issues.
- Activation evidence.
- Acquisition-source record.
- Founder decision.

## Exit Criteria

The product is ready to leave beta when:

- Relevant users can access it.
- Core blockers are understood and addressed to an acceptable level.
- First value can be observed.
- Feedback can be collected reliably.
- Acquisition source is known.
- The founder knows what the next launch experiment is meant to learn.

## AI Role

AI may prepare recruitment copy, organize observations, cluster feedback, detect repeated friction, maintain issue priorities, and summarize acquisition evidence.

AI must not fabricate user behavior or feedback.
