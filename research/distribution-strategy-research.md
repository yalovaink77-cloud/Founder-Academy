# Distribution Strategy — Research Report

## Purpose

Distribution strategy defines how a product consistently reaches customers and converts attention into sustainable business results.

A strong product without distribution has limited impact.

## Core Principles

Distribution should be designed early.

Questions:

- Who is the customer?
- Where do they spend attention?
- How do they discover solutions?
- Who influences their decisions?
- What creates trust?

## Distribution Types

### Organic Distribution

Examples:

- SEO
- Content
- Community
- Word of mouth
- Referrals

Advantages:

- Compounding value
- Lower long-term cost

### Paid Distribution

Examples:

- Search ads
- Social ads
- Sponsorships

Advantages:

- Fast testing
- Immediate reach

Requires:

- Positive unit economics

### Sales Distribution

Examples:

- Founder sales
- Outbound
- Enterprise sales

Advantages:

- High learning
- Direct customer feedback

### Product-Led Distribution

The product itself drives growth through:

- Sharing
- Collaboration
- Invitations
- Network effects

## Distribution Strategy

A strategy should define:

- Target audience
- Primary channel
- Message
- Conversion path
- Retention mechanism
- Measurement system

## Metrics

Measure:

- Acquisition cost
- Conversion
- Activation
- Retention
- Revenue
- Referral

## Common Mistakes

- Building product without distribution.
- Using every channel simultaneously.
- Optimizing views instead of customers.
- Ignoring retention.

## Founder Academy Standard

Distribution is not marketing activity.

Distribution is a system that connects customer problems with valuable solutions.
