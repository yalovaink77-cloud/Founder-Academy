# Positioning — Research Report

## Purpose

Positioning defines how the product should be understood in the customer's mind relative to available alternatives.

The objective is clarity, differentiation, and relevance.

## Core Principles

Positioning is not branding.

Brand expresses identity.

Positioning defines perception.

## Positioning Questions

- Who is the customer?
- Which category do we belong to?
- Which problem do we solve?
- What makes us different?
- Why should customers believe us?

## Positioning Components

### Target Customer

Who is this product for?

### Category

Which market or category does the customer compare us with?

### Alternative

What does the customer currently use?

### Differentiation

Why are we a better choice?

### Proof

What evidence supports our claim?

## Validation

Positioning should be validated using:

- Customer interviews
- Messaging tests
- Landing pages
- A/B tests
- Sales conversations
- Conversion rates

## Common Mistakes

- Trying to appeal to everyone.
- Competing on features.
- Using vague marketing language.
- Ignoring customer language.
- Frequently changing positioning.

## Founder Academy Standard

Good positioning makes the product immediately understandable to the right customer.
