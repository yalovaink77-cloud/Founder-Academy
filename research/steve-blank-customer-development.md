# Steve Blank — Customer Development Research

## Core Model

Customer Development has four stages:

1. Customer Discovery
2. Customer Validation
3. Customer Creation
4. Company Building

Early-stage startups should focus first on Discovery and Validation.

## Customer Discovery

Customer Discovery tests the founder's assumptions about:

- Customer
- Problem
- Product
- Market
- Channel
- Pricing
- Revenue model

The founder must test these assumptions directly with real customers.

The objective is learning, not selling.

## Customer Validation

Customer Validation tests whether the business has:

- Real customers
- A repeatable sales process
- A workable distribution channel
- Evidence of willingness to pay
- A business model capable of repetition

Positive interviews do not complete Customer Validation.

Paying customers and repeatable acquisition provide stronger evidence.

## Core Principles

### There Are No Facts Inside the Building

Business plans begin as assumptions.

Founders must test them outside the company with customers.

### Founders Must Lead Discovery

Customer Discovery should not initially be delegated to:

- Consultants
- Sales teams
- Marketing agencies
- Researchers

Founders need to hear unexpected and negative evidence directly.

### Search Before Execution

A startup is searching for a business model.

It should not behave like an established company executing a proven model.

### Hypotheses Before Experiments

Document assumptions before testing them.

Examples:

- Who is the customer?
- What problem exists?
- Who pays?
- Which channel reaches them?
- Why will they switch?
- What will they pay?

### Iteration and Pivot

Experiments may lead to:

- Minor iteration
- Major pivot
- Stopping the idea

A pivot changes an important business-model assumption while preserving useful learning.

### Preserve Cash During Search

Do not scale hiring, marketing, or operations before validating the business model.

More money can hide weak demand rather than fix it.

## Problem and Solution Discovery

Problem discovery and solution discovery should be separate.

First determine:

- Does anyone care?
- Is the problem important?
- How is it solved today?

Only then test whether the proposed solution creates sufficient value.

## Market Type

Validation differs by market type:

- Existing market
- New market
- Re-segmented market
- Clone market

Entering an existing market requires understanding competitors, channels, pricing, and customer expectations.

Creating a new market requires longer education and adoption cycles.

## B2B Requirement

In B2B, identify separately:

- User
- Buyer
- Decision-maker
- Budget owner
- Influencer
- Blocker

Interest from a user does not prove that the organisation will buy.

## Founder Academy Standard

Customer Discovery tests whether the founder understands the customer and problem.

Customer Validation tests whether customers can be acquired, converted, and monetized through a repeatable process.

Neither stage is complete because people praise the product.

## Actionable Workflow

1. Write business-model hypotheses.
2. Rank assumptions by risk.
3. Design experiments.
4. Speak with real customers.
5. Record evidence.
6. Update assumptions.
7. Build the smallest useful test.
8. Seek behavioral or economic commitment.
9. Iterate, pivot, or stop.
10. Scale only after repeatability is demonstrated.

## What Founder Academy Accepts

- Founder-led discovery
- Hypothesis-driven experiments
- Problem discovery before solution discovery
- Evidence before scaling
- Iteration and pivot
- Repeatable acquisition and payment as validation

## Important Limitation

Customer Development is not a rigid linear process.

Teams may revisit earlier stages as new evidence appears.

The framework reduces uncertainty but does not eliminate it.

## Founder Academy Principle

Startups do not execute known facts.

They search for a repeatable and scalable business model through evidence.
