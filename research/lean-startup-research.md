# The Lean Startup — Research Report

## Core Model

The Lean Startup is designed for products and businesses operating under significant uncertainty.

Its central loop is:

Build  
↓  
Measure  
↓  
Learn

The objective is not to produce more features.

The objective is to learn whether a sustainable business can be built.

## Validated Learning

Progress should be measured by validated learning rather than:

- lines of code,
- features completed,
- hours worked,
- documents produced,
- downloads without meaningful use,
- or activity without evidence.

Learning is validated when an experiment produces evidence that changes confidence in an important business assumption.

## Leap-of-Faith Assumptions

Every new product contains dangerous assumptions.

Typical assumptions include:

- The customer has the problem.
- The problem matters.
- The proposed solution creates value.
- Customers can understand the value.
- Customers can be reached.
- Customers will change their behaviour.
- Customers will pay.
- Customers will continue using the product.

These assumptions should be documented before major development begins.

## Minimum Viable Product

An MVP is the smallest experiment capable of producing meaningful learning about a critical assumption.

An MVP is not:

- a permanently low-quality product,
- a collection of random features,
- an excuse for poor engineering,
- or necessarily a public application.

Possible MVP forms include:

- Landing page
- Prototype
- Demo video
- Concierge service
- Wizard of Oz workflow
- Manual service
- Paid pilot
- Limited beta
- Pre-sale

The correct MVP depends on the assumption being tested.

## Build

Build only what is necessary to test the current assumption.

Before building, define:

- What are we trying to learn?
- Which assumption is being tested?
- What is the smallest valid experiment?
- What behaviour would support the assumption?
- What result would disprove it?

Building without a learning objective creates waste.

## Measure

Measurement must connect user behaviour to the tested assumption.

Useful measures may include:

- Activation
- Retention
- Conversion
- Payment
- Repeat use
- Referral
- Task completion
- Time saved
- Revenue
- Customer acquisition cost

Vanity metrics should not drive decisions.

Examples of vanity metrics:

- Total registrations
- Total page views
- Total downloads
- Social media followers
- Impressions without conversion

## Innovation Accounting

Innovation accounting provides a structured way to measure progress under uncertainty.

The basic sequence is:

1. Establish the current baseline.
2. Run experiments intended to improve it.
3. Compare results with predefined targets.
4. Decide whether to pivot or persevere.

Metrics should be:

- Actionable
- Accessible
- Auditable
- Connected to customer behaviour
- Connected to business assumptions

## Cohort Analysis

Aggregate totals can hide product failure.

Users should be grouped by meaningful cohorts, such as:

- Signup date
- Acquisition channel
- Product version
- Customer segment
- Pricing plan

Cohorts reveal whether newer users behave better than earlier users.

## Pivot or Persevere

After an experiment, the founder must decide:

### Persevere

Continue when evidence supports the current direction.

### Iterate

Make a limited change when the assumption is partly supported.

### Pivot

Change an important part of the strategy while preserving validated learning.

Possible pivots include:

- Customer segment
- Customer need
- Product scope
- Channel
- Revenue model
- Technology
- Platform
- Growth model

### Stop

Stop when repeated evidence shows that the opportunity does not justify further investment.

## Experiment Cycle

Every experiment should contain:

- Hypothesis
- Target customer
- Assumption
- MVP or test
- Metric
- Success threshold
- Failure threshold
- Time limit
- Cost limit
- Result
- Decision
- Learning

Thresholds should be defined before results are observed.

## Founder Academy Standard

The purpose of an MVP is not to launch faster.

The purpose is to learn faster with the smallest responsible investment.

Build-Measure-Learn should be understood as:

Learn what must be tested  
↓  
Build the smallest valid experiment  
↓  
Measure real behaviour  
↓  
Update the decision

## What Founder Academy Accepts

- Validated learning as the unit of progress
- Assumption-driven experiments
- Small tests before large investment
- Behavioural metrics
- Cohort analysis
- Innovation accounting
- Pivot, persevere, iterate, or stop decisions
- MVP as an experiment

## What Founder Academy Rejects

- MVP as an excuse for poor quality
- Building without a defined hypothesis
- Measuring only downloads or traffic
- Changing success criteria after results
- Endless experimentation without decisions
- Scaling before retention and revenue evidence
- Treating every feature release as learning

## AI Era Relevance

AI reduces the cost and time required to produce software.

This increases the risk of creating large amounts of output without validated learning.

AI can assist with:

- Prototype creation
- Experiment design
- Data analysis
- Interview synthesis
- Hypothesis generation
- Rapid iteration

AI cannot replace:

- Real customer behaviour
- Real retention
- Real payment
- Real acquisition
- Real trust
- Real market evidence

## Actionable Workflow

1. Identify the riskiest assumption.
2. Define the evidence required.
3. Select the smallest valid experiment.
4. Define thresholds before testing.
5. Run the experiment with real customers.
6. Measure behaviour.
7. Record validated learning.
8. Decide: persevere, iterate, pivot, or stop.
9. Test the next riskiest assumption.
10. Scale only after repeatable evidence exists.

## Founder Academy Principle

Output is not progress.

Learning supported by real evidence is progress.
