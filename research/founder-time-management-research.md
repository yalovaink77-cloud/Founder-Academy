# Founder Time Management — Research Report

## Purpose

Founder time management defines how entrepreneurs allocate limited attention toward the highest-value activities.

The objective is maximizing learning, progress, and strategic impact.

## Core Principles

A founder's most valuable resource is focused attention.

Time should be allocated according to:

- Impact.
- Urgency.
- Learning value.
- Strategic importance.

## High-Value Founder Activities

### Customer Understanding

Examples:

- Customer interviews.
- User observation.
- Feedback analysis.

### Product Direction

Examples:

- Prioritization.
- Experiment design.
- Decision making.

### Distribution

Examples:

- Sales.
- Partnerships.
- Growth experiments.

### Strategic Thinking

Examples:

- Market analysis.
- Long-term planning.
- Business model decisions.

## Low-Value Activities

Avoid excessive time spent on:

- Administrative tasks.
- Unnecessary meetings.
- Premature optimization.
- Busy work.

## Deep Work

Important founder work requires:

- Uninterrupted focus.
- Clear objectives.
- Protected time blocks.

## Weekly Operating Rhythm

Review:

- What was learned?
- What changed?
- What matters next?
- Which assumptions need testing?

## Common Mistakes

- Confusing activity with progress.
- Reacting all day.
- Ignoring strategic work.
- Too many priorities.
- No review system.

## Founder Academy Standard

Founders should spend the majority of time on activities that reduce uncertainty and create customer value.
