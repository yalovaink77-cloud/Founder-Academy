# Customer Interviews — Research Report

## Purpose

Customer interviews exist to understand reality, not validate ideas.

The objective is to discover:

- Problems
- Behaviour
- Workflows
- Current solutions
- Buying decisions

## Core Principles

Good interviews explore the customer's past.

Bad interviews ask customers to predict the future.

## Objectives

Learn:

- What happened?
- How often?
- Why?
- How is it solved today?
- What does it cost?
- Who decides?
- What alternatives exist?

## Good Questions

- Tell me about the last time this happened.
- Walk me through the process.
- What did you do next?
- Why?
- What was difficult?
- What have you tried?
- How much time did it take?
- How much did it cost?
- What happened when nothing worked?

## Bad Questions

- Would you buy this?
- Do you like this idea?
- Is this useful?
- Which feature do you want?
- Would you pay?

These encourage speculation instead of evidence.

## Interview Rules

- Listen more than you speak.
- Avoid selling.
- Avoid defending ideas.
- Follow interesting details.
- Ask for examples.
- Verify assumptions.
- Record exact customer language.

## Signals

Strong:

- Real stories
- Repeated behaviour
- Existing workarounds
- Money spent
- Time invested

Weak:

- Opinions
- Predictions
- Compliments
- Suggestions

## Common Mistakes

- Talking too much.
- Pitching the product.
- Leading questions.
- Asking hypothetical questions.
- Interviewing only friends.
- Ignoring contradictory evidence.

## Founder Academy Standard

Interviews should reduce uncertainty by discovering real customer behaviour rather than collecting approval.
