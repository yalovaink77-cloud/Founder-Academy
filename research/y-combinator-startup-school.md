# Y Combinator — Startup School Research

## Core Philosophy

Startups succeed by solving important problems for a small group of customers before attempting to serve everyone.

Growth follows product-market fit, not the other way around.

## Build Something People Want

The primary objective is to create something customers genuinely need.

Customer demand is more important than technical complexity.

## Talk To Users

Founders should continuously talk with users.

Objectives:

- Understand problems.
- Observe behaviour.
- Discover objections.
- Learn customer language.
- Identify priorities.

Customer conversations should continue throughout product development.

## Do Things That Don't Scale

Early growth should prioritize learning over efficiency.

Examples:

- Manual onboarding
- Founder-led support
- Personal outreach
- Direct interviews
- Manual product setup

Automation comes later.

## Launch Early

Products should reach users as early as practical.

Early launch creates opportunities for learning.

Waiting for perfection delays evidence.

## Weekly Progress

Every week should produce measurable learning.

Examples:

- New interviews
- New customers
- Improved activation
- Increased retention
- Better conversion
- Faster onboarding

Weeks without learning should be investigated.

## Product-Market Fit

Evidence includes:

- Organic retention
- Repeat usage
- Customer referrals
- Organic growth
- Increasing willingness to pay
- Users disappointed if the product disappears

## Founder Responsibilities

Founders should initially own:

- Customer conversations
- Product direction
- Prioritization
- Early sales
- Customer support
- Product quality

Delegation comes after repeatable systems exist.

## Growth

Growth should follow product quality.

Improve:

- Retention
- Activation
- User satisfaction

before increasing acquisition.

## Metrics

Useful metrics include:

- Activation
- Weekly Active Users
- Monthly Active Users
- Retention
- Referral
- Revenue
- Conversion
- Churn

Avoid vanity metrics.

## Founder Academy Standard

Startups should optimize learning before optimization.

Talk to users continuously.

Improve the product continuously.

Grow only after customers consistently receive value.

## What Founder Academy Accepts

- Build something people want
- Founder-led customer conversations
- Early launch
- Manual work before automation
- Weekly measurable progress
- Product-market fit before aggressive growth
- Continuous iteration

## What Founder Academy Rejects

- Building in isolation
- Delaying launch for perfection
- Scaling acquisition before retention
- Delegating customer learning too early
- Measuring success by downloads alone

## AI Era Relevance

AI accelerates development.

It does not replace:

- Customer understanding
- Trust
- Distribution
- Retention
- Product-market fit

## Actionable Workflow

1. Identify a real problem.
2. Talk to users.
3. Build the smallest useful product.
4. Launch.
5. Measure behaviour.
6. Improve.
7. Repeat every week.
8. Scale only after repeatable demand exists.

## Founder Academy Principle

The fastest-growing startups usually learn faster than competitors.
