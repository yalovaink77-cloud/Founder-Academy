# Founder Equity & Ownership — Research Report

## Purpose

Founder equity strategy defines how ownership is created, shared, protected, and managed throughout the company's lifecycle.

The objective is aligning ownership with contribution, commitment, and long-term company success.

## Core Principles

Equity is not only compensation.

It represents:

- Ownership.
- Responsibility.
- Risk.
- Long-term alignment.

## Founder Equity

Founders should consider:

- Contribution.
- Time commitment.
- Expertise.
- Risk taken.
- Future responsibilities.

## Equal Splits

Equal founder splits can work when:

- Contributions are similar.
- Commitment is similar.
- Long-term expectations align.

Unequal splits may be appropriate when:

- Contributions differ significantly.
- Roles differ.
- Commitment differs.

## Vesting

Vesting protects the company and founders.

Typical structure:

- Equity earned over time.
- Commitment demonstrated gradually.
- Protection against early departure.

## Employee Equity

Stock options or equity incentives can:

- Attract talent.
- Align employees with company success.
- Reward long-term contribution.

## Dilution

External investment reduces ownership percentage.

Founders should understand:

- Current ownership.
- Future rounds.
- Investor ownership.
- Employee option pools.

## Ownership Decisions

Consider:

- Long-term incentives.
- Fairness.
- Company needs.
- Future hiring.
- Investment strategy.

## Common Mistakes

- Splitting equity without discussion.
- Ignoring vesting.
- Giving too much equity too early.
- Treating ownership as status.
- Failing to plan for future hires.

## Founder Academy Standard

Equity should create alignment and reward long-term contribution.
