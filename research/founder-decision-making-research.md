# Founder Decision Making — Research Report

## Purpose

Founder decision making defines how entrepreneurs choose actions when information is incomplete and uncertainty is high.

The objective is making high-quality decisions with limited time and resources.

## Core Principles

Good founder decisions balance:

- Speed.
- Evidence.
- Risk.
- Reversibility.
- Long-term impact.

## Decision Types

## Reversible Decisions

Characteristics:

- Low cost to change.
- Limited downside.
- Can be tested quickly.

Approach:

Move fast.

Examples:

- Landing page changes.
- Small experiments.
- Messaging tests.

## Irreversible Decisions

Characteristics:

- Expensive to change.
- Large consequences.

Approach:

Slow down.

Examples:

- Major hiring.
- Large investments.
- Strategic direction.

## Evidence-Based Decisions

Use:

- Customer data.
- Experiments.
- Financial information.
- Market signals.

Avoid:

- Ego.
- Assumptions.
- Personal preference only.

## Decision Framework

1. Define the problem.
2. Identify assumptions.
3. Gather evidence.
4. Consider options.
5. Evaluate risks.
6. Decide.
7. Measure outcome.
8. Learn.

## Avoiding Bias

Common founder biases:

- Confirmation bias.
- Founder attachment.
- Overconfidence.
- Fear of changing direction.

Countermeasures:

- Seek contradictory evidence.
- Listen to customers.
- Define success criteria before experiments.

## Founder Academy Standard

The founder's job is not to always make correct decisions.

The founder's job is to create systems that increase the probability of good decisions.
