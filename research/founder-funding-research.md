# Founder Funding — Research Report

## Purpose

Founder funding strategy defines how a startup acquires financial resources while preserving flexibility, ownership, and strategic options.

The objective is funding the right stage of growth.

## Core Principles

Funding is a tool.

It should support:

- Customer learning.
- Product development.
- Sustainable growth.
- Strategic goals.

Funding itself is not success.

## Funding Stages

## Bootstrapping

Using:

- Personal resources.
- Revenue.
- Customer payments.

Advantages:

- Ownership preserved.
- Strong discipline.
- Customer focus.

Challenges:

- Limited resources.
- Slower growth.

## Angel Investment

Early external capital from individuals.

Useful for:

- Initial growth.
- Networks.
- Experience.

## Venture Capital

Institutional investment for companies with large growth potential.

Requires:

- Large market.
- Strong growth potential.
- Scalable model.

## Revenue-Based Growth

Using customer revenue to finance expansion.

Advantages:

- Less dilution.
- Customer-driven growth.

## When To Raise

Raise when capital can accelerate a validated opportunity.

Examples:

- Proven demand.
- Repeatable acquisition.
- Product-market fit signals.

## Before Raising

Understand:

- Why money is needed.
- How much is needed.
- What milestones it achieves.
- Alternative options.

## Investor Perspective

Investors evaluate:

- Market.
- Team.
- Problem.
- Product.
- Traction.
- Growth potential.

## Common Mistakes

- Raising before validation.
- Spending investment to discover basic problems.
- Optimizing for valuation only.
- Ignoring dilution.
- Losing strategic control.

## Founder Academy Standard

Capital should accelerate learning and growth, not replace evidence.
