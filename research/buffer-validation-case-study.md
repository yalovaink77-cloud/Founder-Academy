# Buffer — Validation Case Study

## Background

Buffer tested demand before building the product.

The founders wanted to validate whether people would pay for a social media scheduling tool.

## Initial Assumption

People needed a simpler way to schedule social media posts.

## MVP

The first MVP consisted of:

- A landing page
- Product description
- Pricing page

The product did not exist.

## Validation Method

- Landing page
- Pricing page
- Email signups
- Click tracking
- Customer interviews

## Evidence Collected

Evidence included:

- Email registrations
- Pricing-page clicks
- Customer replies
- Interview feedback

The pricing page measured buying intent before development.

## What Worked

- Very low development cost
- Fast validation
- Pricing tested early
- Founder-led customer conversations

## What Failed

Early interest did not validate retention.

Subscription behaviour required later validation.

## Major Improvements

- Functional MVP
- Beta launch
- Paid subscriptions
- Continuous iteration
- Analytics-driven improvements

## Founder Academy Standard

Demand should be tested before implementation.

Pricing assumptions should be tested before product completion whenever practical.

## Actionable Rules

- Test pricing early.
- Measure intent before development.
- Separate curiosity from commitment.
- Continue validating after launch.

## What Founder Academy Accepts

- Landing-page validation
- Pricing-page validation
- Founder-led interviews
- Incremental product development

## What Founder Academy Rejects

- Building first, validating later.
- Assuming email signups equal paying customers.
- Treating landing-page traffic as Product-Market Fit.

## AI Era Relevance

AI makes landing pages and prototypes inexpensive.

Pricing, retention, and payment still require real customer evidence.

## Founder Academy Principle

A pricing click provides stronger evidence than a compliment.
