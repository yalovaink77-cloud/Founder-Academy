# Founder Capability Curriculum

## Status

Founder Academy v1 — Core Curriculum

This document converts the Academy's research and operating system into a practical founder learning path.

---

## Purpose

Founder Academy exists to close the gap between building a product and building a successful business.

The curriculum is therefore organized around founder decisions and evidence, not around courses, tools, or platforms.

The governing rule is:

> Learn the principle, apply it to a real situation, measure the result, preserve the lesson.

---

## How to Use the Curriculum

Work through the stages in order unless a real project has already produced sufficient evidence for an earlier stage.

Do not skip a stage because the product is technically complete.

A stage is complete when its required decision artifacts and evidence exist, not when its reading is finished.

Each stage uses the Founder AI Operating Protocol:

```text
Goal
↓
Relevant Context
↓
Research / Capability
↓
Action
↓
Verification
↓
Founder Decision
↓
Durable Record
↓
Measurement and Learning
```

# Stage 0 — Opportunity Framing

## Founder Question

What problem or opportunity is worth investigating?

## Learn

- Problem-first thinking.
- Assumptions vs evidence.
- Customer/problem separation.
- Opportunity cost.

## Produce

- Problem statement.
- Target customer hypothesis.
- Existing alternatives list.
- Critical assumptions list.
- Kill criteria.

## Exit Criteria

The founder can state who may have the problem, what the problem is, why it matters, and which assumptions must be tested before serious investment.

`99-playbooks/opportunity-validation-sprint.md`

# Stage 1 — Validation

## Founder Question

Is the problem real enough to justify further work?

## Learn

- Evidence quality.
- Behavioral evidence vs opinions.
- Demand signals.
- Problem frequency and severity.
- Willingness to adopt or pay.

## Produce

- Validation evidence log.
- Assumption test results.
- Continue / change / stop decision.

## Exit Criteria

Evidence is strong enough to justify the next experiment, or evidence is weak enough to stop or change direction.

## Playbook

`99-playbooks/opportunity-validation-sprint.md`

## Founder Question

- Alternatives and switching behavior.

- Customer/problem segments.
- Repeated patterns.
- Decision journey.
- Vocabulary bank.

## Exit Criteria

The founder can describe the customer using observed evidence rather than demographic guesses alone.

# Stage Gate Rule

A founder does not move forward because a checklist feels complete.

Move forward when:

- Required evidence exists.
- Critical assumptions are visible.
- The next risk is understood.
- The founder has made the required decision.
- The result is recorded.

Possible stage decisions are:

- Continue.
- Repeat the stage with a sharper hypothesis.
- Return to an earlier stage.
- Change direction.
- Stop the project.

Stopping weak work early is a valid Founder Academy outcome.

---

# Application to Existing Products

A technically mature product does not automatically begin at Stage 7.

For an existing product:

1. Audit prior evidence against the stage exit criteria.
2. Credit what is already proven.
3. Identify the earliest important evidence gap.
4. Start there.

This prevents a finished product from skipping customer, positioning, distribution, or measurement work.

---

# Learning Record

At the end of every meaningful sprint, record:

- What we believed.
- What we did.
- What happened.
- What evidence changed our belief.
- What decision followed.
- What is reusable beyond this project.

Only generalized, validated learning should become an Academy-wide principle.

---

# Founder Academy v1 Curriculum Completion Standard

The curriculum is operational when a founder can enter with either an idea or an existing product and determine:

- Where to start.
- What to learn.
- What action to take.
- What evidence to collect.
- What decision to make.
- What artifact to preserve.
- When to move forward.

This document provides that path.
